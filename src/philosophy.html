A Philosophy of Data

Data-driven decision-making is the cornerstone of modern organisational success. Medium-sized data-oriented companies rely on diverse roles to manage the acquisition, storage, transformation, analysis, and governance of data. While some overlap exists between these roles, each has a unique focus, requiring specific skills and responsibilities. Together, they form a cohesive ecosystem that supports the organisation's data strategy.

Core Roles and Their Distinctions

Data Architect

Background: Typically a senior role requiring deep expertise in database systems, cloud platforms, and data integration. Often comes with experience in system design and large-scale implementations.

Focus: Designs scalable, secure, and efficient data architectures that align with business objectives.

  • Creating architectural blueprints for data systems (e.g., databases, data warehouses, integration layers).
  • Defining data security, governance, and interoperability standards.
  • Advocating for modern methodologies like Data Vault 2.0 and Data Mesh.

Data Governance Specialist

Background: Often comes from compliance, legal, or senior data management roles, with a focus on regulatory frameworks.

Focus: Ensures organisational data is compliant, secure, and of high quality.

  • Establishing data privacy and usage policies (e.g., GDPR, CCPA).
  • Monitoring and improving data quality.
  • Performing data audits and enforcing regulatory compliance.
  • Promoting ethical data practices and fostering a governance culture.

Data Modeler

Background: Combines database administration and software engineering skills, often with formal training in data modeling or schema design.

Focus: Designs logical and physical data schemas that align with business and technical needs.

  • Translating requirements into efficient data models.
  • Documenting relationships, attributes, and dependencies between entities.
  • Ensuring schema designs are scalable and adaptable.
  • Collaborating with engineers to implement models.

Data Strategist

Background: Often from consulting or leadership roles, with a blend of technical and business expertise.

Focus: Aligns organisational data initiatives with long-term strategic goals.

  • Identifying data-driven opportunities for innovation.
  • Crafting data strategies that support business growth.
  • Defining KPIs to measure success.
  • Advocating for data literacy across teams.

Data Solutions Architect

Background: A hybrid role bridging architecture and engineering with expertise in end-to-end solution design.

Focus: Designs practical, scalable, and cost-effective data solutions for specific business problems.

  • Integrating data systems into workflows.
  • Designing optimised data pipelines and scalable platforms.
  • Recommending emerging technologies.

Data Engineer

Background: Typically rooted in software engineering, with an emphasis on data systems.

Focus: Builds and optimises the infrastructure for collecting, storing, and transforming data.

  • Developing robust ETL/ELT pipelines.
  • Managing data storage systems (data warehouses, lakes, lakehouses).
  • Supporting data scientists and analysts by preparing datasets.

Data Scientist

Background: Experts in statistics, machine learning, and programming, often with advanced degrees in related fields.

Focus: Extracts actionable insights and builds predictive models from data.

  • Conducting exploratory data analysis (EDA).
  • Developing machine learning models and algorithms.
  • Communicating insights through reports and visualisations.

Future Trends

Emerging roles like Data Product Managers are reshaping how organisations approach Data Mesh and domain ownership. Evolving architectures, such as Lakehouses, are becoming the de facto standard for unified data management. Increased automation tools like Prefect and Airflow streamline workflows.
